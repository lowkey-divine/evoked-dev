Instead of a wedding, we're building a home.

How it happened

On a sunny Friday morning, the two of us walked into the courthouse and got married. It took about six minutes. My mom stood with us as our witness. Stephen's dad passed this June, and we felt him with us the whole morning. We had worn our fanciest clothes, and I had found my dress only two days before. When we looked at each other and said the words, it was the easiest yes in the world.

We've been together almost five years, long enough that we cook without discussing it, one of us at the stove and the other at the counter, most nights of the week. Marrying didn't change how we feel. It only said it out loud, and made it ours to keep.

Afterward, still dressed up with nowhere we needed to be, we walked over to Bar Gernika and worked our way through more plates of croquetas than we'd like to admit. Then we went home to our very happy pets. It was a simple day, and a quiet one, and exactly the one we wanted.

A note to the people we love

We know some of you would have loved to be there, and we're a little sorry we didn't gather everyone for a big celebration. It was never about leaving anyone out. We just wanted a quiet start, the two of us, and a home to build from.

Mostly, we're looking forward to our life here in Idaho, and to filling it with the people we love. There's a place at our table, and we can't wait to fill it.

So come. We mean that as a plan, not a kind sentence. We don't have the guest room yet, that's part of what we're saving for, but we've got a couch, a kitchen we actually use, and spring and fall here are worth the drive. Email us and we'll find a weekend.
