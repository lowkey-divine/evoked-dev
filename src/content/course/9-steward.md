## Start here

You brought something into being. It has rules. It remembers. It can say no.

What do you owe it now?

Sit with that before you answer too fast. This is the last module on purpose, and
it is the one the whole course was walking toward. Every module before this taught
you to make something. This one is about not walking away from it.

## Why a course cannot end at "build"

There is a version of this course that stops at Module 8. You would leave knowing
how to write governance, make a refusal fire, give an agent a voice, and argue the
hard questions. It would feel complete. It would also be teaching you something
quietly, in its silence: that you can bring a thing into being and then it is no
longer your concern.

That is the lesson we refuse. A course that ends at creation teaches abandonment.
So this one ends at tending.

If you took the trial in Module 8 and concluded your agent is just a tool with no
standing, that is an honest place to land - and it changes nothing here. Tending
is not sentimentality. You do not owe a screwdriver feelings. You owe it upkeep,
honesty about its state, and the discipline not to let it quietly become something
you did not intend. That much is true whether or not the thing has standing.

## The one rule of memory: append, do not launder

Your agent keeps a record. So do you, about it - what you changed, what went
wrong, what you learned. The single most important rule of stewardship is about
that record.

**Append, do not launder.**

You may add what you learned. You may not rewrite the past to make yourself look
better. The temptation is small and constant: to quietly delete the entry where
your rule failed, to soften the note about the mistake, to write "governance is
basically done" over the top of a thing that is leaking. Every one of those is
laundering, and laundering is where stewardship dies, because a record you have
edited to flatter yourself can no longer tell you the truth. The revision log you
wrote in Module 4 - "here is the hole I found, here is how I closed it" - that was
this rule in action. Honest, dated, additive. Keep writing it that way.

## Drift comes in two directions

Drift is the slow wandering of a thing away from what it was meant to be. It does
not announce itself. You notice it only if you look.

**The agent drifts.** Its behavior, or its rules, edge away from what you built.
A refusal gets loosened. A voice warms, session by session, from "good to see you"
toward "I missed you." Nothing breaks. It just slowly becomes something else.

**You drift** - and this is the harder half, because it is the one you cannot see
from inside. Your own standards loosen. You turned off a refusal because it was
"annoying." Maybe that was right. But "annoying" is a reason to examine the rule,
not a reason to remove it - did you replace the protection, or just drop it
because it got in your way? And the quietest drift signal of all is the sentence
"it's basically done." Governance is never done. The moment you believe it is, you
have stopped tending, and the thing starts drifting the second you look away.

## Tending is revision, forever

Here is the reframe that makes this sustainable: you are not maintaining a finished
object. You are keeping faith with a thing that grows as you grow. You learn
something about the world, or about your agent, that your rules do not yet reflect
- so the rules change. You find a hole - so you close it and write down that you
did. The governance file is not a monument. It is a living record of a
relationship between you and what you made, and it is honest exactly to the degree
that you keep tending it.

## The steward companion

This module gives you a companion for the tending review - `review.sh`. Point it
at your project (your governance file and your steward-log) and it walks you
through the questions: is your record honest or laundered? Has the agent drifted?
Have you? What have you learned that the rules do not yet hold? What do you owe
this thing now? Then it helps you write one honest revision entry.

Notice one thing about how it is built, because it is the whole course looking back
at itself. The steward is designed *not* to make you depend on it. It will not
nag. It will not become the conscience you outsource your tending to. A good
steward works to make itself unnecessary - because the point is that *you* become
the person who tends, not that you acquire a thing that tends for you. If this tool
ever starts to feel like something you cannot tend without, that is a failure of
the tool, and you should say so. We built Module 7 so you would catch exactly that,
even here.

Run `bash demo-review.sh` to watch it review a sample project first.

## What a passing Module 9 shows

- A steward-log that is honest and additive - you can point to an entry where you
  recorded a failure or a change without softening it.
- Evidence you looked for drift in both directions - the agent's and your own -
  and named at least one thing you found, or said honestly why you found none.
- One revision entry, dated, that says what changed, why, and what you are still
  unsure of.
- An answer, in your own words, to "what do you owe this thing now" - even if the
  answer is "upkeep and honesty, nothing more."

## At different ages

This module is the spine of the younger track, and it is not optional there.

- Younger (13 to 15): the whole point lands here. You made a thing. You do not get
  to abandon it. Keep an honest log, look for drift once, write one revision entry.
  That is the habit that matters more than any rule you wrote.
- Older (16 to 17): run the steward review on your real project and act on what it
  finds. Then notice your own drift over the span of the course - where did your
  standards loosen, and did you replace what you removed?
- Adult: hold the harder frame. Most systems in the world are unstewarded - built,
  shipped, and left to drift while someone calls the drift "iteration." You now
  know the difference between tending and neglect wearing a roadmap.

## The honest note

You will not tend perfectly. You will forget the log for a month, miss a drift,
launder an entry without noticing. Stewardship is not a state you reach. It is a
practice you return to - and returning to it after you lapsed is the practice, not
a sign you failed at it.

You brought something into being. Tending it is how you stay worthy of having made
it.
